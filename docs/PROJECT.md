# OneColor — Full Project Brief

OneColor Website: UI/UX Designer Brief
Nov 16, 2026 · @Avi Sharma
Goal and audience
Design a Shopify website for OneColor, a premium hair colour with one product in several shades, that converts visitors into buyers and feels like a full brand, not a one-product page.
	•	Product: a permanent hair colour in a one-click bottle that releases colour cream and developer together, with no manual mixing. The client describes it as a German-engineered dual-chamber bottle with a 1:1, no-waste ratio.
	•	Name: sold as OneColor in India because of trademark registration, and as Keralock in Europe. Do not use the Keralock name or the European site's visuals.
	•	Range: 5 shades at launch, growing to 9. Each shade gets its own product page, not one page with colour variants.
	•	Price: ₹2,000 MRP, with a 10-15% discount online. It is positioned as premium, alongside brands like Just Herbs.
	•	Audience: Indian customers aged about 23 to 60, from early grey-hair users to long-term colourers.
	•	Platform: Shopify only. Mobile-first, because most Indian traffic is mobile.
Design direction
Make the site vibrant, warm and aspirational. The client said the European black-and-white, minimalist look feels dated to Indian audiences, and that the site must visually beautify the product to drive purchase intent.
	•	Avoid: the European Keralock look, which is minimal, black-toned and clinical.
	•	Tone reference: Dream Beauty, for how a small catalogue gives room to storytelling and brand heritage through bold colour and beauty-led photography.
	•	Imagery: stylish beauty shots of Indian women and men across the 23-60 age range, with close-ups of real hair colour results. No stock-looking studio silos.
	•	Colour: a warm base palette, with each shade's own colour used as the accent on its product page. Suggested; the designer can propose alternatives.
	•	Hero object: the circular bottle, shown large, with the one-click activation as a recurring visual.
	•	Voice: premium but friendly, not clinical. Keep copy short and scannable on mobile.
Pages and deliverables
The priority is a homepage and one reusable shade product page, designed mobile first, with desktop adapted from them.

Shade product page: built to convert
Design one template that answers every purchase doubt above the fold and keeps the buy button in reach. Each of the shades gets its own page, so the template must work with different imagery and copy per shade.
	•	Gallery: the shade on real hair, before and after, the bottle, and a short application video.
	•	Price block: ₹2,000 MRP, with the 10-15% online discount shown as a clear saving.
	•	Shade switcher: a swatch row to compare shades without going back to the shop page.
	•	Sticky add-to-cart bar on mobile, visible while scrolling.
	•	Benefit icons: no mixing, no waste, and the other approved claims, kept to four or five.
	•	How it works strip: the one-click bottle in three short steps.
	•	Trust strip: delivery, COD and returns, plus a pin-code delivery check.
	•	Reviews with photos, placed high on the page.
	•	FAQ accordion for patch test, grey coverage and how long the colour lasts.
	•	Cross-sell: a complementary product or bundle, which also adds order value for a one-product brand.
A single-product brand has no catalogue to compare against, so shoppers decide on trust and proof. Keep the page fast: compress beauty imagery and use lazy loading below the fold.
Justifying the higher price
At ₹2,000 MRP the site has to prove value before the shopper reaches the price, so build the case into the layout instead of leaving it to one line of copy. Claims marked with * need client approval before they go on the site.
	•	Show the technology early. The one-click bottle that mixes colour and developer is the main reason to pay more, so give it its own homepage section and a strip on every shade page.
	•	Show what is in the box. The European site includes a comb and conditioner. Show them as a "what you get" block next to the price.
	•	Make the benefits visible. Icons for no mixing, no mess, no waste, and for vegan, ammonia-free, pH-neutral and reusable up to 4 months*.
	•	Compare, don't brag. A "Why OneColor" table against box dye and salon visits on mess, time and waste. Add cost per use only if the reuse claim is confirmed*.
	•	Frame the discount. Show ₹2,000 struck through beside the online price so the 10-15% saving is obvious.
	•	Lean on proof. Put results photos and reviews close to the price block, and add ingredients and patch-test guidance so the premium feels safe.
Customer reviews
Reviews are the main trust signal for a hair colour, so design them as a system that appears on the homepage, every shade page and a dedicated results page.
	•	Summary near the price: star rating and review count as a tappable link to the reviews.
	•	Photo and video reviews first, because results on real hair convince more than text.
	•	Filters: by shade and hair type, plus a "verified buyer" mark.
	•	Results gallery: before and after shots across ages 23-60, linked back to the matching shade page.
	•	Themes from Europe: reviewers there praise ease of use, colour result and the pre-mixed format. Reflect these as short highlight chips, and write fresh Indian reviews instead of copying European ones.
	•	Known complaints (gloves tear, box hard to open) can be answered with a short "how to open and use" clip near the reviews.
	•	Low-review state: the Indian brand is launching, so also design the page for when there are few reviews, for example with the results gallery and expert or founder notes, so it never looks empty.
